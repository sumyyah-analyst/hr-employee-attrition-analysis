# HR Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview

This portfolio project provides an end-to-end data analysis of workforce turnover using the **IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset**. The goal of this analysis is to identify key patterns behind employee attrition, uncover workforce trends, and deliver data-driven recommendations to help leadership improve employee retention.

The project covers a complete analytics pipeline—from data cleaning and exploratory analysis in Excel and SQL to building an interactive Power BI dashboard and presenting strategic business recommendations.

## Business Problem

Employee turnover creates significant operational challenges, including high recruitment costs, loss of institutional knowledge, and reduced team productivity.

This analysis addresses key organizational questions:

- What is the current headcount, overall attrition count, and attrition rate?
- Which departments and specific job roles experience the highest employee turnover?
- How do demographic factors such as age group and gender relate to attrition trends?
- Does working overtime relate to employee exits?
- What role do income levels and work-life balance play in employee retention?

---

## Dataset Summary

- **Source:** IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance
- **Volume:** 1,470 employee records across 35 features
- **Target Variable:** `Attrition` (Yes / No)
- **Key Attributes:** Demographics, compensation, overtime status, work-life balance scores, departments, and job roles.

---

## Key Insights

- **Overall Metrics:** Out of 1,470 employees, 237 have left, representing a **16.12% attrition rate**.
- **Department Concentration:** **Research & Development** recorded the highest total exits (133), followed by **Sales** (92). However, attrition rate is also analyzed by department to account for differences in department size.
- **Age Demographic:** The **26–35 age group** represents the largest attrition cohort with 116 exits.
- **OverTime:** Employees working overtime show a substantially higher level of employee exits compared with non-overtime staff.
- **Work-Life Balance:** Employees with a work-life balance score of **3** accounted for the largest exit volume (127 employees).

---

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Target Retention in High-Exit Departments:** Conduct workload assessments and career-progression reviews within R&D and Sales teams.

2. **Mitigate Overtime Burnout:** Re-evaluate scheduling and staffing levels for high-turnover roles, such as Laboratory Technicians and Sales Executives, to reduce workload pressure and potential burnout.

3. **Early-Career Engagement:** Implement targeted mentorship and professional growth opportunities for employees in the 26–35 age bracket.

4. **Strengthen Work-Life Balance:** Investigate workload, management support, and employee engagement among groups showing higher levels of attrition.
